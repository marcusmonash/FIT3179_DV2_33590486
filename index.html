Visitor Spending In Australia

1. Where Visitors Come From

Overview of international visitor travel route, stay and transport.

  • New Zealand has the greatest volume of international visitors to Australia.
  • Business travelers have the highest daily spending ($1,299/trip) across 3.1 days.
  • Holiday visitors average 3.3 days with good spending ($1,144/trip).
  • Education & VFR has the longest stays with lowest daily expenditure.
  • Domestic air travel has the greatest expense of $28.8B.
  • Road transit (vehicle fuel & hire cars) is the largest expenditure on the road.
  • Local transit overall presents the smallest share of tourist transport spending.

2. Where the Money Is Spent?

Geographic breakdown of tourist spending by state and international spending

  • New South Wales has the highest total tourist spending in Australia at $65.9 billion.
  • Eastern states (NSW, VIC, QLD) receive over 78% of all tourist spending in Australia.
  • Smaller territories like Northern Territory ($2.6B) have the lowest total tourist spending.
  • United Kingdom ($8.6B) and USA ($8.1B) bring in the most spending from international visitors.
  • Japan ($7.6B) leads Asian countries in tourist spending, ahead of China ($3.4B).
  • New Zealand ($5.6B) relatively high total spending can be attributed to the many visitors arrive from there.

3. How Tourist Spend Their Money?

Breakdown of tourist spendings, state category spending and comparison of local and international spending.

  • Dining out takes up the largest share of tourist spending at 23.0% ($32.5B).
  • Long-distance travel (19.8%) and groceries (18.5%) are the next biggest expenses.
  • Taxis and rideshares account for the smallest share of spending at 1.6%.
  • NSW dining has the highest spending in a single category ($10.2 billion).
  • Queensland leads long-distance travel ($8.2B), spending more than both NSW and Victoria.
  • Eastern States (NSW, VIC, QLD) spend the most across all expense categories.
  • Domestic travellers account for over 75% of spending in every major category.
  • International tourists spend the largest share of their money on food and drinks (23.1%).
  • Dining out depends mostly on local travellers, who account for 86.7% of spending.

4. How has Spending Changed?

Tracking tourist spending across time.

  • Pandemic impact (2020–21): Spending on long-distance travel fell by 67.6% due to border closures.
  • Recovery after 2022: Spending on dining out ($32.1B) and accommodation ($21.8B) more than doubled compared with before the pandemic.
  • Long-term change: Dining out grew to become Australia's biggest tourist expense.
