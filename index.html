Visitor Spending In Australia

Understanding Visitor Behaviour, Spending Habits and Economic Benefits in Australia

1. Global Journey: Who Visits Australia?

A breakdown tracking international visitor arrival routes, average stay lengths and preferred transport categories across Australian destination cities.

  • New Zealand has the greatest volume of international visitors coming into Australia.
  • Japan brings in the highest number of asian visitors about 834,000 coming into Brisbane.
  • Main Entry Cities: Most visitors travelling from far away arrive through either Sydney and Melbourne.
  • Business travelers have the highest daily spending ($1,299/trip) across 3 days.
  • Holiday visitors average 3 days with good spending ($1,144/trip).
  • Education & VFR has the longest stays with lowest daily expenditure.
  • Domestic air travel has the greatest expense of $28.8B.
  • Road transit (vehicle fuel and petrol) is the largest expenditure on the road.
  • Local transit overall presents the smallest share of tourist transport spending.

2. Money Flow: Where Tourism Dollars Come And Go

Geographic breakdown showing how visitor spending is distributed across Australian states and comparing total spending by tourist origin countries.

  • New South Wales has the highest total tourist spending in Australia at $65.9 billion.
  • Eastern states (NSW, VIC, QLD) receive over 78% of all tourist spending in Australia.
  • Smaller territories like Northern Territory ($2.6B) have the lowest total tourist spending.
  • United Kingdom ($8.6B) and USA ($8.1B) bring in the most spending from international visitors.
  • Japan ($7.6B) leads Asian countries in tourist spending, ahead of China ($3.4B).
  • New Zealand ($5.6B) relatively high total spending can be attributed to the fact of its proximity so more people visit therefore higher spending.

3. What Tourists Spend On: Spending Choices Breakdown?

An analysis of what visitors buy across major spending categories, state expense category breakdown, and comparison between domestic and international spending.

  • Dining out takes up the largest share of tourist spending at 23.0% ($32.5B).
  • Long-distance travel (19.8%) and groceries (18.5%) are the next biggest expenses.
  • Taxis and rideshares account for the smallest share of spending at 1.6%.
  • NSW dining has the highest spending in a single category ($10.2 billion).
  • Queensland leads long-distance travel ($8.2B), spending more than both NSW and Victoria.
  • Eastern States (NSW, VIC, QLD) spend the most across all expense categories.
  • Domestic visitors account for over 75% of spending in every major category.
  • International visitors spend the largest share of their money on food and drinks (23%).
  • Dining out depends mostly on domestic visitors, who account for 87% of spending.

4. Times Change: How has Spending Changed?

A decade timeline tracking tourist spending also looking at the impacts of the pandemic.

  • Pandemic impact (2020–21): Spending on long-distance travel decreased by 67.6% due to COVID restrictions.
  • Recovery after 2022: Spending on dining out ($32.1B) and accommodation ($21.8B) more than doubled compared with before the pandemic.
  • Long-term change: Dining out grew to become Australia's biggest tourist expense.

5. The Big Picture: Economic Impacts

Analysis of economic output against jobs created and visitor spending based on visitor profiles.

  • Top 3: New South Wales, Victoria and Queensland generate $52.18B total (77.9%) of Australia's tourism income and provide 406,100 (79.5%) total of direct tourism jobs.
  • Top State: New South Wales leads Australia in tourism income ($20.53B) and provides over 152,400 direct tourism jobs.
  • Bottom 2: The Northern Territory ($0.83B, 4,500 jobs) and ACT ($1.30B, 7,800 jobs) have lower tourism income and fewer jobs because of smaller populations and land.
  • Business Travel: Business travellers spend the largest percentage of their budget on accommodation (42.0%).
  • VFR Visitors: Visitors visiting friends and relatives spend more on shopping, which make up 32.0% of their budget.
  • Holdaymaker: Holidaymakers diverse their spending across different categories, with accommodation (28.5%) and dining out (26.0%) being the highest percentages.
